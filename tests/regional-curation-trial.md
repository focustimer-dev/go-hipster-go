# Regional curation trial — 2026-10-24

## Result and limits

Four three-record candidate sequences were researched against the proposed regional atlas. All twelve artist/title/release identities below have artist or label catalog support. This was a source-based curation trial, not an audio listening session: mood, perceived pace, sonic hooks not explicitly documented, and the actual transitions remain provisional. No Spotify/YouTube availability, saved playlist, BPM, listening-history exclusion or comparative improvement over the previous skill is claimed. No publishing was attempted.

Three possible research directions were considered per brief. The chosen directions below are hypotheses, not automatic selections. No random surprise was invoked: these are fixed evaluation fixtures rather than ordinary GO playlists.

## 1. Oceania / sad / slow — Rooms After Everyone Leaves

Compared: Hawaiian vocal/hymn material; Māori lament repertoire; contemporary Aotearoa intimate songwriting. Chose the third for this sample because direct lyrics and release provenance were accessible; did not presume hymns or ceremonial songs fit sadness.

1. **Tiny Ruins — The Oldest Bar in Town**, bonus recording on the anniversary edition of *Some Were Meant for Sea*. [Artist release](https://tinyruins.bandcamp.com/album/some-were-meant-for-sea), [artist lyrics](https://www.tinyruins.com/the-oldest-bar-in-town). The text supports estrangement and memory. The exact bonus edition matters; do not assume it occurs on every edition of the original album. Vocal/acoustic arrangement and slow pace require a recording check rather than extrapolation from album tags.
2. **French for rabbits — Claimed by the Sea.**, *Claimed by the Sea EP* version. [Artist track and lyrics](https://frenchforrabbits.bandcamp.com/track/claimed-by-the-sea-2). Artist credits identify voice, guitar and keyboards; the text depicts a lost domestic space. Select this EP recording, not the separately listed remix or a single with a different duration.
3. **Nadia Reid — Richard**, *Preservation* album version. [Artist recording](https://nadiareid.bandcamp.com/track/richard), [artist recording provenance](https://nadiareid.com/pages/music). The artist's discography locates this album's sessions in Lyttelton, New Zealand, despite the current Bandcamp profile saying UK. A regional connection needs release evidence, not the profile location alone. Hold the strict slow/mood fit pending audio and stronger song-level interpretation; do not use comments from Bandcamp customers as artist notes.

Proposed arc: personal distance → lost home → direct relationship confrontation. This is a narrative ordering hypothesis, not a verified audio transition.

**Finding:** useful candidate set, but all three selections cluster in New Zealand English-language songwriting. The Oceania references did not by themselves prevent a familiar research corridor. This is a discovery-breadth limitation, not a reason to impose a geographic quota or force a lament into the playlist.

## 2. Central America / happy / brisk — Nobody Consulted the Seating Plan

Compared: cimarrona ensemble releases; Garifuna guitar/song recordings; Costa Rican independent psych/funk-pop. Chose a Garifuna-to-Costa-Rica bridge instead of three entries from the same label ecosystem.

1. **AURELIO — Yalifu**, *Garifuna Soul* version, catalog duration 3:59. [Artist album](https://aureliomusic.bandcamp.com/album/garifuna-soul), [official artist music page](https://www.aureliomusic.net/music). A paranda-related vocal/guitar route is supported at artist/album level. The *Darandi* recording is separately listed at 4:27; it is not an interchangeable exact match. Do not infer happy lyrics or brisk pace solely from the genre.
2. **Chella Torres & Desere Diego — Mérua**, original *Umalali: The Garifuna Women's Project* recording. [Credited track](https://garifunacollective.bandcamp.com/track/m-rua), [label project notes](https://www.stonetreerecords.com/music/umalali/). Preserve the individual artist credits rather than reducing every track on the project to the catalog account name. Label credits specifically identify tenor saxophone on this track; do not extend every project-level instrument credit to this recording.
3. **Cocofunka — Chúcaro**, *Chúcaro* album version. [Artist track and lyrics](https://cocofunka.bandcamp.com/track/ch-caro), [Costa Rican cultural directory](https://si.cultura.cr/agrupaciones-y-organizaciones/cocofunka). This adds a separately documented Costa Rican scene instead of another Garifuna-label selection. The playful/untamed reading is a tentative interpretation; the lyric includes darker images too. Exact briskness and the transition from Mérua need audio review.

Proposed arc: song-led opening → expanded ensemble → contemporary psych/pop turn. Do not sell this as a seamless mix before hearing it.

**Finding:** looking beyond the first label produced a credible alternative research door. Happy must describe the musical experience requested, not an invented translation. Cimarrona remains a useful directory route but this trial did not establish a compelling exact cimarrona recording; do not fill the slot with generic festival-band repertoire.

## 3. Central Africa / weird / mid — The Wiring Has Opinions

Compared: Bulu mvet storytelling; Chadian balafon ensemble recordings; contemporary Kinshasa/Brazzaville acoustic-electronic hybrids. Chose contemporary hybrids to avoid defaulting to archive material as a shortcut for Weird.

1. **Les Mamans du Congo & Rrobin — Ngaminke**, original self-titled album recording. [Artist track](https://lesmamansducongo.bandcamp.com/track/ngaminke). Credits support the collaborators, and the project documents a meeting of Congolese vocal repertoire and electronic/hip-hop production. That project description does not prove every instrument or texture on this track.
2. **KOKOKO! — Tokoliana**, *Tokoliana / L.O.V.E.* version. [Artist track notes](https://kokokomusic.bandcamp.com/track/tokoliana). The artist describes production with punk/funk character and a subject of social struggle/corporate greed. This supplies a better factual hook than calling unfamiliar vocals strange. It does not independently establish a specific crime narrative.
3. **Kin'Gongolo Kiniata — Toye Mabe**, *Kiniata* version. [Artist track](https://kingongolokiniata.bandcamp.com/track/toye-mabe). The track notes describe an energetic welcome, and the band documents its handmade-instrument practice. Do not invent the exact materials heard on this track. The energy may overshoot Mid; hold until listening.

Proposed arc: voice/electronics → song-shaped punk/funk → more forceful ensemble entrance. The last energy step is the likely failure point.

**Finding:** the route can reach documented contemporary work across both Congos and multiple release organizations. It can also collapse into the familiar exported Kinshasa scene. KOKOKO!'s debut single is a touchstone, not evidence of a deep discovery; retain at most as an anchor while researching less predictable alternatives for a full set.

## 4. Southern Africa / weird / fast — Agricultural Policy for the Dance Floor

Compared: Oviritje local releases; Shambo-derived electronic collaboration; Durban gqom/sgubhu. Chose a source-supported Namibian-to-Durban production bridge. Namibia and South Africa are inside the requested Southern African research area; the parent UI zone remains Central & Southern Africa.

1. **One Joe x Okey Szoke — Efiku**, *One Joe EP* recording. [Label release and track-specific notes](https://brothersisterrecords.bandcamp.com/album/one-joe-ep). The label explicitly describes a Shambo-derived Namibian collaboration, electronic production drawing on quicker-paced styles, and this song's narrative of an underestimated orphan succeeding through education. Australian production collaboration does not erase the documented Namibian vocal/scene connection.
2. **DJ Lag — 16th Step**, *GCA006 - DJ Lag EP* version. [Label release](https://gooncluballstars.bandcamp.com/album/gca006-dj-lag-ep). The label distinguishes this track's sgubhu approach from Ice Drop's gqom character. Keep that distinction; genre labels alone still do not establish perceived pace.
3. **Dominowe — Realm Of Zardoz**, *Seippelabel Vol. 14* recording. [Label track](https://seippelabel.bandcamp.com/track/realm-of-zardoz). The label independently documents the artist's Durban connection. The label's Beijing location is not the recording's geographic scene. Hard-hitting producer biography is not enough for track-level anger, speed or production claims; hold those until audio is checked.

Proposed arc: melodic digital vocal piece → club-centered rhythmic pivot → a further Durban club candidate. This is the riskiest proposed transition of the four; it must survive actual playback, otherwise split the research routes instead of defending a clever idea.

**Finding:** Shambo yielded a substantive primary release source rather than only a category name. Oviritje remains thinner in this trial: category recognition and press leads did not yet become a sufficiently documented exact recording shortlist. Do not confuse the failure to resolve a candidate here with absence of a scene or lack of musical value.

## Negative control: Criminal

None of these twelve candidates is accepted as Criminal on the evidence collected. Corporate greed in Tokoliana, surreal police imagery in Chúcaro, and a hardship-to-success narrative in Efiku do not by themselves demonstrate the required explicit crime-centered song narrative. Likewise gqom intensity and any scene's reputation cannot qualify a recording. Do not widen the geography or quietly relax the evidence gate to finish twelve songs.

## Decisions before release

- Keep the atlas as an addressable research map. A source label is a research door, not a substitute for song judgment.
- Improve Shambo's evidence with the One Joe release above. Keep Oviritje's category-only claim bounded and continue artist/label research before treating that cue as comparably actionable.
- For full playlists, research beyond these anchors, labels and export-facing scenes. Do not turn this test's twelve recordings into a mandatory roster.
- Require an audio review of perceived tempo, specific musical hook and each adjacent transition before calling any of these sequences successful. Identity verification is not a listening test.
- A future before/after comparison needs the same fixed briefs, verification access and listening conditions. This trial does not measure improvement against the old skill.
