# Go Hard — Deep Crate Protocol

**Activation:** Only explicit `go hard`, `go funky hard`, `go hard slow`, a clear natural-language request for Go Hard, or the PWA's `Curation mode: GO HARD` activates this mode. Treat `hard` as a **curation-effort** modifier, not a mood, genre, tempo or loudness cue. It composes with regions, mood, tempo, count, service, and optional cover art. **Default OFF:** Ordinary `go` and all other requests keep the existing fast single-pass selection/sequencing, source budget and publishing path. A text mention of "hard rock" does not activate the mode.

**After the same cheap writer preflight**, expend additional effort on editorial quality, not filler:
1. **Deep candidates:** For a normal twelve-track playlist, aim to evaluate roughly **36–50 distinct plausible recordings** across relevant scenes, eras, labels and unexpected sonic links, verifying actual artists/recordings and observing the user's region and content constraints. For other requested counts, scale the pool sensibly. This is a *research target*, not permission to fabricate entries, exceed available tools, or delay publishing indefinitely; use a smaller credible pool when coverage is limited.
2. **Evidence-backed fingerprints:** For shortlisted songs, record concise sourced or explicitly tentative observations about groove/pulse, rhythm, instrumentation, ensemble/vocal role, texture, production density, dynamics and (when supported) harmonic color. Exact tempo, meter, key, and intro/outro behavior require trustworthy track-specific evidence or actual available audio analysis; **never pretend to have listened**. Avoid expensive audio feature extraction unless the host genuinely exposes it and it meaningfully helps the decision.
3. **Competing journeys:** Select the requested number of verified final recordings, draft **up to three distinct orders** using rhythmic, timbral or narrative continuity, and compare all adjacent handoffs. Prefer a convincing worst transition, a satisfying long-form arc, and genuine discovery over sameness or an artificial energy graph. Do not present the three drafts as separate playlists.
4. **Adversarial edit:** Challenge the weakest handoff and any overreliance on genre labels, familiar artists, repetitive textures or unearned contrasts. If an exciting cross-scene jump needs a bridge, allow **one targeted bridge-track search** and a supported replacement; never impose bridge quotas. Make **at most two targeted revision rounds**, checking every newly created neighbor and artist-spacing/count/region constraints. Stop early when evidence cannot support an improvement. Keep purposeful surprises that survive scrutiny.
5. **Publish once, honestly:** Deliver and attempt to publish the **single best final sequence**, retaining the requested track count and exact verified recordings/order when the music-service writer permits. Perform normal count/readback checks. If the provider cannot write a precise list, disclose that limitation; never claim a playlist was saved solely because the deep editorial pass succeeded.

The normal GO rule against extra music research, numerical scoring and second sequencing passes remains in force **unless Go Hard was explicitly invoked**. Go Hard permits bounded deeper exploration and comparison, but still requires honest evidence, sensible resource use and a complete result rather than endless deliberation.
